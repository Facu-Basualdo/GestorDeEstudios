# Software Safety

> Software safety is a software quality assurance activity that focuses on the identification and assessment of potential hazards that may affect software negatively and cause an entire system to fail.

Hazards are identified and categorized by criticality and risk. For example, hazards associated with a computer-based cruise control include: (1) causes uncontrolled acceleration, (2) does not respond to brake pedal, (3) does not engage, (4) slowly loses or gains speed. Analysis techniques such as fault tree analysis, real-time logic, and Petri net models are used to predict the chain of events that can cause hazards and the probability of occurrence. Once hazards are identified, safety-related requirements can be specified. Software safety differs from software reliability: reliability uses statistical analysis to determine likelihood of failure, while safety examines how failures result in conditions leading to mishaps, evaluated in the context of the entire system.
